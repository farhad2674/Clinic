# Scene diversity and brainstorming

Read the page before inventing a visual metaphor. A medical term does not require a syringe, vial, microscope, glass orb, or pseudo-scientific sculpture. Service portraits do not depict results.

## Three different concepts per job

Each concept names setting, action, framing, light, palette, and styling/materials. Change the visual idea, not only garment color. For a skin-quality guide:
1. Environmental portrait in cool architectural shade, terracotta shirt, woman turning toward window light.
2. Seated waist-up portrait in an olive blouse on a plum chair, quietly closing a cosmetic bag.
3. Close three-quarter portrait against rose plaster, textured bob and mineral-teal sleeve, relaxed direct gaze.

Select by relevance, crop safety, and contrast with neighboring images. For a product article the alternatives might be a bathroom-shelf moment, a tactile cotton/cream study, and a travel washbag scene.

| Scene family | Believable moment | Variation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skin care | Closing a jar, gathering a washbag, drying hands | Side daylight, overhead detail, shoulder-level view |
| Hair care | Holding a comb, gently parting hair, hair settling | Different hair silhouette, profile, back three-quarter |
| Consultation preparation | Closed folder, glasses, blank notes | Seated, standing arrival, overhead objects |
| Dressing area | Adjusting a sleeve, picking up a bag, turning from mirror | Warm wood, cool painted wall, cropped reflection |
| Architectural shade | Relaxed stance, short walk, doorway pause | Light direction, plaster plane, unequal negative space |
| Simple portrait set | Thoughtful gaze, listening off-frame, seated shift | Rose plaster, slate wall, understated colored paper |
| Skincare still life | Relevant containers, cotton, cloth, modest cream amount | Table-edge view, overhead scene, material close-up |
| Editorial workspace | Blank paper, calendar without readable labels, glasses | Surface, arrangement, scale, daylight direction |
| Conceptual interior | Practical reception/waiting area | Furniture spacing, tactile materials, restrained greenery |

Use medical/research objects only when directly useful to the article and plausible. Avoid unsupported microscopic imagery presented as evidence. Disease articles should use restrained conceptual context rather than fabricated diagnostic references or treatment outcomes.

## Compare the collection

Track setting, action, framing, light direction/temperature, palette, identity, hair silhouette, wardrobe, and props. Neighboring images should differ across several axes; three is a useful review prompt rather than a mechanical rule. Separate service portraits have different identities. Do not silently repeat a service model in unrelated editorial photographs.

Compare eye line, shoulder/head angle, hand placement, camera distance, and subject scale. Rotate portraits and still lifes according to topic. Most scenes need zero to three purposeful props. Do not turn a white jar, beige towel, plant, sphere, or headscarf into a universal signature.

## Crop safety

Article covers display at desktop 4:5, square mobile, and 4:3 landscape cards. Keep eyes/topic cues inside their common useful region and allow room around hair, shoulders, and hands. Parallax needs edge clearance. Determine focal position from the actual photo.

A landscape interior inside a tall arch needs a deliberately composed vertical crop. Review it at the actual placement; a good landscape master may fail in that mask.
